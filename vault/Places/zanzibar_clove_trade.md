The Zanzibar clove trade refers to the 19th-century spice plantation economy built around the island of Zanzibar and the coastal wafwasi (Arab, Indian, and Persian planters) of the Swahili coast. Under Omani rule from 1698 and especially after Sultan Said bin Sultan relocated his capital to Zanzibar in 1840, the island became the world's leading center of clove production. Labor for the plantations was supplied in large part by enslaved and semi-enslaved East African workers, and the clove trade was tightly linked to the Indian Ocean slave trade. Free capping (clipping) of cloves, export to the Persian Gulf and later to Europe, and the 1880s abolition of the slave trade under Sultan Barghash reshaped the economy, which remained dominant in the island's economy into the early 20th century.
