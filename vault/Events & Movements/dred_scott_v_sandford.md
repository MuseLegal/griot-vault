*Dred Scott v. Sandford*, 60 U.S. (19 How.) 393 (1857), was a U.S. Supreme Court case in which the Court, in an opinion authored by Chief Justice Roger B. Taney, ruled that enslaved and free Black people were not and could not be citizens of the United States under the Constitution, and that Congress had no power to prohibit slavery in the federal territories. The decision, issued March 6, 1857, invalidated the Missouri Compromise's prohibition on slavery north of the 36°30′ parallel and deepened sectional conflict. Dred Scott, an enslaved man who had lived for a time in the free state of Illinois and the free Wisconsin Territory, had sued for his own and his family's freedom; the Court's holding that he lacked standing to sue in federal court made the ruling a national landmark of constitutional failure, later effectively overruled by the Thirteenth and Fourteenth Amendments.
