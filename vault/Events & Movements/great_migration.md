The Great Migration (1910–1970) was one of the largest movements of people in United States history. Roughly six million African Americans left the rural and urban South for industrial and commercial cities in the Northeast, Midwest, and West, in two broad waves: the First Great Migration (1910–1940) and the Second Great Migration (1940–1970). Push factors included Jim Crow segregation, economic hardship, and racial violence; pull factors included wartime labor shortages, higher wages, and the prospect of escape. Cities such as New York, Chicago, Detroit, Philadelphia, and later Oakland and Los Angeles saw dramatic shifts in their Black populations, transforming both the urban landscape and the cultural life of the North and West.
