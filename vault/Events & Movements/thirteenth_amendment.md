The Thirteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution, passed by Congress on January 31, 1865, and ratified by the requisite three-fourths of the states on December 6, 1865, abolished slavery and involuntary servitude except as punishment for a crime. Its Section 1 — "Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude, except as a punishment for crime whereof the party shall have been duly convicted, shall exist within the United States, or any place subject to their jurisdiction" — ended the legal institution that had held an estimated 4 million Black people in bondage. The amendment was the legislative capstone of the Civil War and the first of the three Reconstruction Amendments, establishing the constitutional foundation for the subsequent Fourteenth and Fifteenth Amendments.
