## Summary

Sundiata Keita (c. 1217–1255), also known as Mari Djata or Lion King, was the founder of the Mali Empire and one of Africa's most celebrated historical figures. After defeating the Sosso king Soumaoro Kanté at the Battle of Kirina in 1235, Sundiata established an empire that stretched across much of West Africa. His story, preserved in the *Epic of Sundiata*, is one of Africa's most important oral traditions and a foundational narrative of West African civilization.

## Early Life: The Cursed Prince

Sundiata was born around 1217 to Naré Maghán Konaté, the king of the Mandinka people, and Sogolon Kedjou, a bull elephant woman (so named for her physical strength and appearance). According to the epic, Sundiata was born with disabled legs and could not walk until age seven.

His stepmother, Sassouna, and her sons persecuted Sundiata and his mother. After his father's death, Sundiata and Sogolon were exiled from the Mali kingdom. They wandered for years, eventually settling with Sogolon's brother in the forest kingdom of Yaya.

## The Walking Miracle

At age seven, Sundiata saw a wooden cradle made of ironwood (a sacred tree) and declared he could walk on it. He pulled himself up, breaking the bindings that had kept his legs bound, and walked for the first time — a miracle that demonstrated his destiny.

He then forged an iron-tipped bow and spear, becoming a skilled hunter and warrior. His strength and courage attracted followers, and he became a legendary figure.

## The Battle of Kirina (1235)

Sundiata returned to Mali with an army of exiled peoples and defeated Soumaoro Kanté, the Sosso king who had conquered much of West Africa. The Battle of Kirina (1235) established Sundiata as the dominant power in the region.

Soumaoro Kanté was said to possess magical powers, including the ability to turn people to stone. Sundiata's victory was achieved through courage, strategy, and the support of the people he liberated.

## Empire Building

After Kirina, Sundiata built the Mali Empire:
- **Territory:** Stretched from the Atlantic coast to modern-day Niger, including parts of Mali, Guinea, Senegal, Gambia, and Burkina Faso.
- **Government:** Established the *Gbara*, a grand assembly of representatives from all parts of the empire.
- **Constitution:** The *Kouroukan Fouga* established laws governing social order, resource distribution, and human rights.
- **Economy:** Controlled gold fields and trans-Saharan trade routes.

## The Epic of Sundiata

The *Epic of Sundiata* is an oral tradition passed down by griots (traditional storytellers) for centuries. It was recorded in written form by Djéli (griot) Dité Camara in the 1950s. The epic includes:

- Sundiata's miraculous birth and childhood disability
- His exile and return
- The Battle of Kirina
- His establishment of the Mali Empire
- Moral lessons about courage, perseverance, and leadership

The epic was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2009.

## Legacy

Sundiata Keita's legacy includes:
- Founding the Mali Empire, one of Africa's greatest civilizations.
- The *Epic of Sundiata*, recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
- The *Kouroukan Fouga*, one of the earliest known constitutions in human history.
- Being a symbol of African resistance, courage, and leadership.
- His story continues to be told by griots across West Africa.

## Significance

Sundiata Keita represents the pinnacle of pre-colonial African state-building and cultural achievement. His empire laid the foundation for centuries of West African political, economic, and cultural development. The *Epic of Sundiata* remains one of Africa's most important literary and cultural artifacts, preserving the values and history of the Mandinka people.
