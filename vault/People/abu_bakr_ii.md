Mansa Abubakari II (also called Ibn Aminadani) was, according to an account recorded by the 14th-century Moroccan geographer Ibn Battuta, the ruler of the Mali Empire who in 1324 launched a trans-Atlantic expedition — a fleet reportedly numbering in the hundreds of ships — with the stated aim of sailing beyond the Pillars of Hercules (the Strait of Gibraltar) and, upon the return of part of the fleet, of leading a second voyage to cross the Atlantic. Ibn Battuta, who met Abubakari in the Mali capital Niani that same year, is the sole primary source for the story; modern historians treat the trans-Atlantic episode with caution, noting that the account was written from memory decades after the fact and that no shipwrecks or landing sites have been confirmed.
