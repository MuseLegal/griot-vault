## Summary
Nzinga Mbande (1583–1663) was the ruler of the Ndongo and Matamba kingdoms (in present-day Angola) for 39 years (1624–1663). She resisted Portuguese colonial expansion and the transatlantic slave trade for over four decades, negotiating with Portuguese authorities, forming strategic alliances, and leading military campaigns. She is one of the most celebrated figures in African history for her diplomatic skill and military genius.

## Context
Nzinga was born into the royal family of Ndongo. Her father, King Kilange, was forced to sign a treaty with Portugal in 1575, making Ndongo a Portuguese vassal state. Nzinga learned Portuguese, Christianity, and diplomacy from early contact with Portuguese traders. When her brother Ngola Mbandi died in 1624, she seized power. She negotiated with Portuguese authorities in Luanda (1622), famously insisting on being received on equal terms — when offered a mat to sit on the floor, she had a servant kneel instead.

## Significance
Nzinga resisted Portuguese colonization for 40 years, forming alliances with the Dutch (who occupied Luanda 1641–1648) and leading military campaigns. She converted to Christianity strategically (Kimbanguism) to strengthen diplomatic ties while maintaining traditional practices. Her reign demonstrates African agency in resisting European colonialism. She is remembered as a symbol of African resistance and female leadership.
