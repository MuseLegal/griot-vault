## Summary

William Edward Burghardt Du Bois (1868–1963) was an American sociologist, historian, civil rights activist, Pan-Africanist, and writer. He was the first African American to earn a Ph.D. from Harvard University (1895) and became one of the most important intellectuals and activists of the 20th century. He co-founded the NAACP (1909), edited its magazine *The Crisis*, and organized the Pan-African Congresses. His seminal work *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903) remains a foundational text of African American literature and sociology.

## Early Life and Education

Born February 23, 1868, in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, to Alfred and Mary Silvina Du Bois. His father abandoned the family when Du Bois was an infant. He was one of the few Black students at Fisk University (Nashville, Tennessee), where he was exposed to the realities of Southern racism.

His educational achievements were unprecedented:
- **1890:** Bachelor's degree from Fisk University, summa cum laude.
- **1890:** Bachelor's degree from Harvard University.
- **1895:** Ph.D. from Harvard University — the first African American to earn a doctorate there.
- **1892–1894:** Graduate study at the University of Berlin, where he was influenced by German historicism and sociology.

## The Souls of Black Folk (1903)

*The Souls of Black Folk* is Du Bois's most influential work, a collection of 14 essays that established the concepts of:

- **"Double consciousness":** The sense of always looking at oneself through the eyes of others, of measuring one's soul by the tape measure of a world that looks on in amused contempt and pity.
- **"The color line":** The problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the color line.
- **The Talented Tenth:** The idea that the top 10% of Black Americans should lead the race toward progress.

The book combined scholarship, autobiography, and social criticism to argue for full civil rights and political equality for African Americans.

## Atlanta University and Sociology

Du Bois served as a professor of history, economics, and sociology at Atlanta University (1897–1910), where he conducted pioneering sociological research:

- **The Philadelphia Negro (1899):** The first systematic sociological study of an African American community in the United States.
- **The Negro's Land-Cholding Association (1900):** Documented Black land ownership patterns.
- **The Negro American Family (1908):** Studied Black family structures.
- **The Negro American Criminal (1904):** Analyzed crime statistics and their social causes.

His research at Atlanta University produced a series of monographs that established sociology as a rigorous academic discipline.

## NAACP and The Crisis

Du Bois co-founded the NAACP (1909) and served as Director of Publicity and Research. He edited *The Crisis* magazine (1910–1934), which became the most influential African American publication of the era:

- Circulation grew to over 100,000 copies per month by 1920.
- *The Crisis* published works by Langston Hughes, Countee Cullen, and other Harlem Renaissance writers.
- Du Bois used the magazine to expose lynchings and advocate for civil rights legislation.

## Pan-Africanism

Du Bois organized five Pan-African Congresses (1919, 1921, 1923, 1927, 1945):

- **1919 Paris Congress:** Presented a memorandum to the Paris Peace Conference demanding self-determination for colonized peoples.
- **1927 Brussels Congress:** Focused on economic exploitation of Africa.
- **1945 Manchester Congress:** Called for African independence and led to the independence movements of the 1950s and 1960s.

## Later Life and Exile

Du Bois's views grew increasingly radical in his later years:
- He joined the Communist Party USA in 1961 at age 93.
- He was arrested and convicted in 1951 for failing to register as a "foreign agent" (later overturned on appeal).
- He renounced his U.S. citizenship in 1961 and moved to Ghana, where he participated in editing the *Encyclopedia Africana*.
- He died in Accra, Ghana, on August 27, 1963, the day of the March on Washington.

## Legacy

W.E.B. Du Bois's legacy includes:
- Founding the NAACP, the most important civil rights organization in American history.
- *The Souls of Black Folk*, a foundational text of African American literature and sociology.
- Pioneering sociological research on African American communities.
- Organizing the Pan-African Congresses, which inspired African independence movements.
- His concept of "double consciousness" remains central to discussions of racial identity.

## Significance

W.E.B. Du Bois represents the intellectual and activist pinnacle of the African American struggle for equality. His scholarship established sociology as a rigorous discipline, his activism founded the modern civil rights movement, and his Pan-African vision inspired African independence. His work remains essential to understanding race, identity, and social justice in the United States and globally.
