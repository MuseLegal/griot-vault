## Summary
Queen Amina (c. 1533–1610), born Aminatu, was the ruler of Zazzau (later Zaria) in present-day northern Nigeria. She reigned for 34 years (1576–1610), expanding the Hausa territories to their largest historical extent. She personally led an army of 20,000 warriors, built fortified walls around cities, and established tributary states stretching from the Atlantic coast to the River Niger.

## Context
Amina was the daughter of King Nikatau of Zazzau and Queen Bakwa Turunku (r. 1536–c. 1566), one of the few female rulers (sarauniya) of Zazzau. She received extensive military training, distinguishing herself as the lead warrior in her brother's cavalry. After her brother's death in 1576, she was appointed ruler. Within three months, she launched her first military campaign. She refused marriage proposals, including from the Emir of Kano, and ruled independently.

## Significance
Amina's conquests made Zazzau the largest Hausa state in history. She built fortified city walls (ditches and ramparts) that still bear her name ("Amina's walls"). Her 34-year reign was marked by near-constant warfare and expansion. She is commemorated with an equestrian statue at Nigeria's National Theater in Lagos. Her story inspired the fictional character Xena, though historians note significant differences.
