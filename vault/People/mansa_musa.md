## Summary
Musa I of Mali (r. 1312–1337), known as Mansa Musa, was the ninth mansa (king) of the Mali Empire. His 1324–1325 pilgrimage to Mecca made the Mali Empire known across North Africa, the Middle East, and Europe. Arabic chroniclers al-'Umari and Ibn Khaldun documented his caravan, which included thousands of attendees and 80 camels carrying gold, causing a temporary devaluation of gold in Egypt.

## Context
Musa inherited the Mali Empire, which controlled major gold-producing regions (Bambuk, Bure) and trans-Saharan trade routes. He expanded the empire to include Timbuktu and Gao. His pilgrimage to Mecca and Medina (1324–1325) traveled over 4,000 miles, spending lavishly in Cairo, which drew European cartographers' attention — by 1375, the Catalan Atlas depicted Musa holding a gold nugget.

## Significance
Musa's pilgrimage put Mali on medieval world maps. He built the Gao and Timbuktu mosques, endowments that made Timbuktu a center of Islamic scholarship. Two-thirds of medieval Mediterranean gold came from West Africa, largely under Mali's control. His reign demonstrates that pre-colonial Africa controlled the world's largest gold supply.
