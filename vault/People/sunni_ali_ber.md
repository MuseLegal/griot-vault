## Summary

Sunni Ali Ber (r. 1464–1492) was the founder and first great king of the Songhai Empire, one of the largest and most powerful states in African history. A military genius, he expanded the empire across the Sahel by conquering Timbuktu (1468) and Djenne (1473), incorporating major centers of Islamic scholarship and trans-Saharan trade into his realm.

## Historical Context

Born around 1430, Sunni Ali inherited a modest Songhai kingdom centered on Gao. Through relentless military campaigns, he transformed it into an empire stretching from modern-day Niger to the western reaches of the Niger River. His reign marks the golden age of the Songhai Empire, which would later be surpassed by Askia Muhammad's administrative reforms.

## Military Conquests

- **Timbuktu (1468):** Captured from the Tuareg, Sunni Ali recognized the city's strategic and economic value as a hub of the trans-Saharan gold and salt trade.
- **Djenne (1473):** Conquered this major trading and scholarly center, securing control over the Niger River trade routes.
- **Futa Tooro (c. 1470s):** Expanded westward into the Futa region, defeating the ruling Berghab dynasty.

His military innovations included a powerful navy operating on the Niger River and a cavalry force that dominated the Sahel. He personally led campaigns and was known for his tactical brilliance.

## Relationship with Islamic Scholars

Sunni Ali's relationship with Timbuktu's Islamic scholars was complex and often hostile. He persecuted some scholars, burning books and destroying mosques, which led to accusations of un-Islamic behavior. However, he respected the spiritual power of local African religious practitioners (fulani marabouts) and incorporated indigenous spiritual practices alongside Islam. This dual approach reflected the syncretic nature of Songhay society.

## Legacy

Sunni Ali's conquests laid the foundation for the Songhai Empire's golden age. His empire stretched from the Atlantic coast to central Niger, making it the largest state in West African history at that time. After his death in 1492 (possibly by drowning in the Niger River), his son Muhammad I briefly ruled before being overthrown by Askia Muhammad, who would further expand and administratively organize the empire.

## Significance

Sunni Ali's military genius and territorial expansion made the Songhai Empire the dominant power in West Africa, surpassing the declining Mali Empire. His control of Timbuktu and Djenne ensured that these centers of Islamic scholarship and trade would flourish for decades, influencing the cultural and intellectual development of the Sahel region.
