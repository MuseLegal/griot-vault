## Summary

Queen Nzinga Mbande (c. 1583–1663) was the ruler of the Kingdoms of Ndongo and Matamba (in modern-day Angola) from 1624 to 1663. A brilliant diplomat and military strategist, she resisted Portuguese colonial expansion for four decades, forming alliances with the Dutch, the Kalunga people, and runaway slave communities. She is remembered as one of Africa's most formidable anti-colonial leaders.

## Early Life and Rise to Power

Born around 1583 to King Kimpa Mbande of Ndongo (in modern Angola), Nzinga was educated in diplomacy and warfare. Her father sent her to negotiate with Portuguese traders at Luanda, where she demonstrated remarkable political acumen by converting to Christianity (taking the name Ana de Sousa) to strengthen ties with the Portuguese.

After her brother King Ngola Mbande died in 1624, Nzinga seized power, becoming one of the few African women to rule a major kingdom in her own right.

## Resistance to Portuguese Colonialism

### The Luanda Negotiation (1622)
Nzinga traveled to Luanda to negotiate with Portuguese Governor João Correia de Sousa. When the Portuguese refused to offer her a seat (forcing her to stand), she had a servant form a human stool, demonstrating her refusal to be diminished. She negotiated favorable terms, including the right to practice Christianity in Ndongo.

### Military Campaigns
- **1624–1627:** Allied with the Dutch West India Company to attack Portuguese settlements.
- **1627–1630:** Defeated Portuguese forces at the Battle of Mbwila.
- **1630–1640:** Established a fortress at Matamba, using it as a base for guerrilla campaigns.
- **1641–1648:** Allied with the Dutch against the Portuguese, then switched sides when the Dutch were expelled.

### Alliance Building
Nzinga formed strategic alliances with:
- The Kalunga people, who resisted Portuguese rule.
- Runaway slave communities (quilombos), who provided military support.
- The Dutch West India Company, who supplied weapons and ships.

## Legacy

Queen Nzinga's resistance to Portuguese colonialism lasted nearly 40 years, making her one of the longest-serving anti-colonial leaders in African history. She maintained her kingdom's independence through diplomacy, military strategy, and religious flexibility (converting to Christianity when useful, then returning to traditional beliefs).

Her legacy includes:
- Being depicted in Angolan currency and monuments.
- Inspiring African feminist movements and anti-colonial struggles.
- Representing the resilience of African kingdoms against European colonialism.
- Serving as a model of female leadership in African history.

## Significance

Queen Nzinga represents one of Africa's most important anti-colonial resistance movements. Her four-decade struggle against Portuguese expansion demonstrates the sophistication of African statecraft, military strategy, and diplomatic skill in the face of European colonialism. Her legacy as a female leader in a patriarchal society continues to inspire African women and global feminist movements.
