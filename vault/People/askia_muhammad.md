## Summary
Askia Muhammad (Muhammad Ture), known as Askia the Great, ruled the Songhai Empire from 1493 to 1528. After defeating Sunni Baru in 1493, he established the Askia dynasty and transformed Timbuktu into a golden age of Islamic scholarship. He undertook a pilgrimage to Mecca (1496–1497), sought Islamic scholarly legitimacy, and organized the empire's administration with ministers for finance, justice, agriculture, and fisheries.

## Context
Askia Muhammad was a general under Sunni Ali Beer, who had sacked Timbuktu in 1468 and persecuted its scholars. After seizing power, Askia reversed this policy, patronizing Muslim scholars, rebuilding mosques, and establishing Qur'anic schools (madrasas). His 1496–1497 pilgrimage to Mecca solidified his Islamic credentials. Under his rule, Timbuktu housed 150–180 Qur'anic schools with 4,000–5,000 students, public libraries, and scholars who matched or surpassed learning in the broader Islamic world.

## Significance
Askia Muhammad's reign (1493–1528) marked the Golden Age of Timbuktu. He organized one of the most sophisticated administrative systems in pre-colonial Africa, with specialized ministries. His patronage of Islamic scholarship made Timbuktu a center of learning in theology, astronomy, mathematics, and law. His reign ended with the Moroccan invasion of 1591.
